The Writings of
Richard Burton

Collected Meditations & Exhortations
Chapter I

Why We DeFi

Bitcoin gave the people a place to hold their wealth. Ethereum gave them a place to build their contracts. And from those contracts grew an open source financial system — a living organism composed of communities forging tools for all. Written as the GameStop saga shook Wall Street, Burton draws a line from the Linux revolution to decentralised finance: both are peaceful uprisings of open code against closed power. “Why Occupy Wall Street? DeFi Wall Street Instead.”

Chapter II

The Etherean & American Dream

America was born by telling the British monarchy to jog on. Ethereum was born by telling centralised servers to do the same. Burton draws a sweeping parallel between these two frontier cultures — both reward the adventurous, forgive honest failure, and back unproven dreamers. Both are credibly neutral substrates upon which anyone may build. “If your smart contract has a dumb bug that costs everyone hundreds of millions, we all learn together.”

“The best Americans and Ethereans are crazily adventurous. They fund wild charitable experiments, back the most unproven founders, and get involved in some of the most extreme sporting activities.”

Chapter III

Ethereum’s Moat Is Its Culture

Every great castle needs a moat, and Ethereum’s is not built of water but of people. Burton identifies three pillars of defence: obsessive developer service, an opt-in communal culture where the best contributions come from the unlikeliest places, and a deep care for cultural expression through tasteful tokens. “Open Source is entirely Opt-In. You can have a full-time team working all hours get absolutely schooled by someone in their spare time.”

Chapter IV

Open Source Monetary Policy

Fewer than one thousand souls control the monetary pulse of the entire planet — the governors of the Federal Reserve, the Bank of England, the European Central Bank, and the People’s Bank of China. Bitcoin proposed a radical experiment: print twenty-one million coins across a hundred and forty-four years and see what happens. But the world’s monetary woes demand something more complex, more open, more communal.

Chapter V

Social Networks & Sociable Protocols

When Facebook added tagging to images, it sparked the social networking revolution. When Ethereum added the NFT standard to images, it sparked the sociable protocol revolution. Burton envisions a future where connections between people are owned by the people themselves — not by corporations. Where earning surpasses liking. Where communities choose their own algorithms. “Earning is way more powerful than getting likes.”

Chapter VI

Token Tests

Four market cycles have taught Burton a bitter lesson: token trading rewires the brain for failure. Trading algorithms are worse than feed algorithms, getting young men addicted to leverage they cannot handle, killing their creativity entirely. His counsel, hard-won: when speculators are manic, buy something real; when they are depressed, send money to ethical founders. “Stop trading shitcoins and start creating tasteful tokens.”

a warning to the young

Chapter VII

The Liquidity Liability

In the old world, startup employees clutch illiquid options for decades, praying for an exit. In the new world, protocol contributors can access liquidity within months. But Burton warns that instant liquidity is not always a blessing — it can be a curse. Illiquidity, paradoxically, can be a feature: it forces patience, commitment, and long-term thinking upon those who hold.

Chapter VIII

Tokens’ Powers

If you own any crypto at all, ask yourself this: which token will you never sell? Burton shares his answer — genesis Ether, a valentines NFT minted for a friend using Balance, objects imbued with memory and meaning far beyond market price. These are not assets. They are relics. “I am never selling. I am not for sale.”

“Those will be collectors’ items in the future but I am never selling them.”

Chapter IX

People, Protocols & Private Keys

A seed phrase can generate an infinite number of Ethereum addresses — think of it as a single key that opens multiple locks to different homes. Burton maps the future of wallets: one interface for many chains, colours and patterns to make network selection explicit, and a radical simplicity that keeps the conceptual model clear. One seed phrase, one wallet, one person.

Chapter X

Token Gated Spaces

Your wallet can unlock doors both digital and physical, with tokens acting as the key. Burton chronicles a new architecture of access: the $BAE crew gating events with crowdfunded ERC20 tokens, Guild.xyz creating token-gated channels, OurSpaces.xyz bridging private online clubs with real-world venues. Digital objects now grant passage to places otherwise unreachable.

✸ ✸ ✸
Chapter XI

Token Gated Messaging

Discord, Telegram, Discourse — all were built by Web 2 founders for a Web 2 world. They do not consider how tokens change everything. Burton envisions messaging where tokens prove genuine interest rather than mere claim. Where holders of governance tokens may read developer discussions, and only the committed may reply. “People prove their interest rather than just claiming it online.”

on gated discourse

Chapter XII

PadawanDAO

One man does not scale. A community does. After years of personally funding young people’s journeys to Ethereum conferences, Burton watched the kids from Lisbon form PadawanDAO themselves — using the very technology they had come to learn. Donors become Jedi Masters. The apprentices become the teachers. The cycle continues.

“If you have made money from Ethereum,
help teach the next generation.”

Chapter XIII

Direct Messages Are Anything But

Perhaps ten million messages have been directed his way across a lifetime of screens — AOL IM, MSN, Slack, Discord, Twitter, iMessage. And yet so rarely are they truly direct. They are indirect, laden with miscommunication. A direct message is a route to your neural pathways: someone loading letters into your eye sockets. With age, Burton has learned to close that door and attend only to what he loves.

✿ · ✿ · ✿
Chapter XIV

Say It To My Face

They diddle out little text messages, write sub-tweets, send emails, mention him in private Telegram groups — but they will never say it to his face. When Rainbow forked away from Balance, the team communicated by email, timed during his grandmother’s funeral while he was in the United Kingdom. This is a raw call for directness, for courage, for the ancient human dignity of looking someone in the eye.

on courage & cowardice

Chapter XV

Dropping Out & Dropping In

Dropping out of Edinburgh University was easy — it took days. Dropping in to the right community took years. From selling his apparel company Hoodeasy, to teaching himself Ruby on Rails, to joining Stripe, to finding Ethereum, Burton traces the winding path that no institution could have charted. The leaving is quick. The arriving is the life’s work.

“Dropping out of Edinburgh University was easy — it took days.
Dropping in to the right community took years.”
